Workload-Aware Adaptive Buffer Pool Management

Empirical Database Research · PostgreSQL 17 Validation · Dynamic Multi-Policy Arbitration

1. Project Mission & Foundational Problem

Modern database management systems (DBMS) such as PostgreSQL rely on static buffer replacement algorithms—most commonly Clock-Sweep (a circular-queue approximation of Second-Chance LRU) or classical LRU. While performant for general-purpose transactional workloads, static algorithms fundamentally suffer from pathological degradation under mixed or phase-shifting HTAP (Hybrid Transactional/Analytical Processing) workloads:

The Scan Pollution Vulnerability

A single bulk analytical table scan (e.g. SELECT * FROM orders) floods the buffer pool with one-touch pages, evicting frequently accessed index pages and hot transactional rows. Static LRU takes hundreds of accesses to recover its working set.

Frequency Stagnation

Static LFU maintains high hit rates for Zipfian skewed queries, but fails completely when workload regimes transition: stale pages with accumulated historical counts remain permanently cached, blocking newly active working sets from residence.

The Thrashing Threat

Naive adaptive cache systems that switch policies frequently suffer from thrashing: the transient cold misses and reorganization overhead during policy transition can exceed the benefit of the new policy, deteriorating overall throughput.

3. Mathematical Formulations & Policy Decision Engine

I. Normalized Shannon Entropy
$$H(W) = -\frac{1}{\log_2 |U|} \sum_{p \in U} P(p) \log_2 P(p)$$

Where $U$ is the set of unique pages in window $W$. When $H \to 0$, access is concentrated on a small set of hot pages (favoring LFU). When $H \to 1$, access is uniform.

II. Jensen-Shannon Divergence
$$D_{\text{JS}}(P \parallel Q) = \frac{1}{2} D_{\text{KL}}(P \parallel M) + \frac{1}{2} D_{\text{KL}}(Q \parallel M)$$

Where $M = \frac{1}{2}(P + Q)$. Measures distribution drift between window $W_t$ and $W_{t-1}$. A spike above threshold $\tau = 0.28$ indicates a workload phase boundary.

III. Cost-Benefit Switching Gate
$$\text{NetBenefit} = \Delta H_{\text{exp}} - \left( \alpha \cdot \frac{B}{B_{\text{ref}}} \right) \ge \gamma$$

Where $B$ is buffer capacity, $B_{\text{ref}} = 64$, and $\gamma \ge 0.020$. Ensures policy switches are mathematically profitable before committing.

4. Replacement Policy Taxonomy

LRU (Least Recently Used)
Time Complexity: $\mathcal{O}(1)$ with doubly-linked list

Evicts the page whose last access timestamp is oldest. Optimal for strong temporal locality. Vulnerable to sequential scan pollution.

LFU (Least Frequently Used)
Time Complexity: $\mathcal{O}(1)$ with frequency bucket list

Evicts the page with the lowest cumulative access count. Optimal for Zipfian hot-page distributions. Suffers from frequency stagnation when workloads shift.

FIFO (First-In First-Out)
Time Complexity: $\mathcal{O}(1)$ with circular queue

Evicts the oldest page resident in the buffer pool. Immune to scan pollution during table scans because non-reused pages pass through without evicting hot sets.

PostgreSQL 17 Clock-Sweep
Time Complexity: Amortized $\mathcal{O}(1)$ circular scan

Maintains usage counts (0 to 5) per frame. A rotating hand decrements counts until finding a zero-count victim. The real-world production baseline.
